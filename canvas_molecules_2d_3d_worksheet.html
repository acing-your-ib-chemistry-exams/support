Molecules and ions in 2D and 3D: predicting the angles

Eighteen species. Each shows the formula, the structural formula, the 2D view and the 3D view. You work out the angles.

Safety

No chemicals are used. A pencil and an eraser are all you need.

Learning objectives

You can:

  • match the formula, the structural formula, the 2D view and the 3D view of the same species;
  • read a wedge-and-dash drawing;
  • name the shape at an atom and give the size of each marked angle.

Key ideas

The 2D view is the displayed formula. It shows every atom and every bond, drawn flat on the page, so the angles on the page are not the real angles. The 3D view shows the real shape. In the 3D view:

LineWhat it shows
plain linea bond lying in the plane of the page
solid wedgea bond coming towards you
dashed wedgea bond going away from you

Ions have square brackets and an overall charge in the corner. A small + or − beside an atom is the charge on that atom. A letter such as a on a 3D view marks an angle you must work out. A flat species, such as carbon dioxide or sulfur trioxide, looks the same in both views because every atom lies in the plane of the page.

  2. Count the atoms joined directly to it. A double bond joins one atom, so count it once.
  3. Count the double bonds on it in the 2D view.
  4. Read the shape and the angle from the chart.

Underlying ideas: definitions to learn

Formula
The number of atoms of each element in one molecule or ion.
Structural formula
The atoms written in the order they are joined, with groups kept together.
Displayed formula
A flat 2D drawing of every atom and every bond.
3D view
A drawing that uses plain lines, solid wedges and dashed wedges to show the real shape.
Ion
An atom or group of atoms with an overall charge.
Bond angle
The angle between two bonds that meet at the same atom.
Shape
The 3D arrangement of the atoms joined to a central atom.
