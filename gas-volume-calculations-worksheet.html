Gas Volume Calculations

Moles ↔ volume of a gas at room temperature and pressure (RTP)

This worksheet covers the two calculations in Edexcel's Moles, Formulae and Equations workbook, Exercises 4c and 4d: finding the volume of a given number of moles of gas, and finding the number of moles in a given volume of gas. Work through the cheat sheet and worked examples first, then try the practice questions — each one marks itself as soon as you type an answer.

Cheat sheet: the key idea

Avogadro's Law tells us that it does not matter which gas you have — at room temperature and pressure (RTP), one mole of any gas occupies the same volume: 24 000 cm³ (24 dm³). That single fact is all you need for both directions of this calculation.

V = n × 24 000 n = V ÷ 24 000

(V in cm³, n in mol — if a question gives the volume in dm³ instead, use 24 in place of 24 000.)

Notice both equations use the same 24 000 — one is just the other turned round. If you can do one, you can do the other.

Worked example 1: moles → volume

Calculate the volume, in cm³, occupied by 0.1 moles of NH₃ at RTP.

We are given an amount in moles, so we are finding a volume — use V = n × 24 000.

V = n × 24 000 V = 0.1 × 24 000 V = 2400 cm³

Worked example 2: volume → moles

Calculate the number of moles in 500 cm³ of NH₃ at RTP.

We are given a volume, so we are finding an amount in moles — use n = V ÷ 24 000.

n = V ÷ 24 000 n = 500 ÷ 24 000 n = 0.0208 mol
Think carefully before calculating. Has the question given you an amount in moles (so you are finding a volume), or a volume (so you are finding an amount in moles)? Decide which direction you're going before you pick up the calculator.

Glossary

Mole (mol)
The chemist's "counting unit" for atoms, molecules or ions — one mole is 6.02 × 10²³ particles.
RTP (room temperature and pressure)
The standard conditions these calculations assume: about 20 °C and normal atmospheric pressure.
Avogadro's Law
Equal volumes of any gas, at the same temperature and pressure, contain equal numbers of moles — so the gas's identity never changes the 24 000 cm³ figure.
Molar gas volume
The volume occupied by one mole of any gas under stated conditions — 24 000 cm³ (24 dm³) at RTP.
