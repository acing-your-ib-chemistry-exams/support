First-order reactions: exponential decay, half-life and the rate constant

Brilliant blue dye and bleach (sodium chlorate(I)), followed by colorimetry

Learning objectives and key ideas

By the end of this sheet you can:

  • recognise a first-order reaction from a graph, because its half-life stays constant;
  • find the half-life from a curve and calculate the rate constant, k, from it;
  • use the exponential decay equation to predict the absorbance at any time;
  • show that ln(absorbance) against time is a straight line whose gradient is −k;
  • explain, using collisions and the Maxwell–Boltzmann curve, how temperature, concentration and a catalyst change the rate.

Key ideas

The colorimeter’s absorbance is proportional to the concentration of the coloured dye, so a falling absorbance shows the dye being used up.

In a first-order reaction the rate at any moment is proportional to how much reactant is left:

rate of reaction = rate constant, k × concentration of dye

Part D: the rate constant, k

The half-life and the rate constant are linked:

The number 0.693 is the natural logarithm of 2, written ln 2. A pure number over a time in seconds gives a unit of “per second”, written s⁻¹.

Part E: the exponential decay equation

The curve you drew follows this equation:

absorbance, A = starting absorbance, A₀ × e−(rate constant, k × time, t)

Starting absorbance, A₀, is the reading at time zero. The rate constant, k, is in s⁻¹ and time, t, is in s.

Worked example (different numbers). A dye solution starts at absorbance 0.90 with rate constant 0.0173 s⁻¹. Find the absorbance after 100 s.

Step 1: rate constant, k × time, t = 0.0173 × 100 = 1.73

Step 2: e−1.73 = 0.177 (press ex then (−) then 1.73)

Step 3: absorbance, A = 0.90 × 0.177 = 0.16

Part F: a straight line from a curve

Taking the natural logarithm of both sides of the equation gives:

ln(absorbance, A) = ln(starting absorbance, A₀) − rate constant, k × time, t

This has the form of a straight line, with ln(absorbance) on the vertical axis and time on the horizontal axis. The line starts at ln(starting absorbance, A₀) and its gradient is −k.

Underlying ideas: definitions to learn

termdefinition
absorbancea measure of how much light a coloured solution absorbs; it is proportional to the concentration of the coloured substance.
first-order reactiona reaction whose rate is proportional to the concentration of one reactant, so the half-life is constant.
exponential decaya quantity that falls by the same fraction in each equal interval of time, following A = A₀ e⁻ᵏᵗ, written in words in Part E.
half-life, t½the time for the concentration of a reactant to fall to half its value.
rate constant, kthe constant in rate = k × concentration; for a first-order reaction it has units s⁻¹ and equals 0.693 over the half-life.
pseudo-first-orderbehaving as first order because the other reactant is in such large excess that its concentration stays almost constant.
activation energy, Eathe minimum energy colliding particles need for a reaction to occur.
Maxwell–Boltzmann distributionthe spread of energies of the molecules in a sample at a given temperature; the area under it is the total number of molecules.
catalysta substance that speeds up a reaction by providing a route with lower activation energy, and is not used up.
