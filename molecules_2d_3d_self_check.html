Molecules and ions in 2D and 3D: predicting the angles

Eighteen species. Each shows the formula, the structural formula, the 2D view and the 3D view. You work out the shape and the angle, check, and use the hints when you need them.

Safety

No chemicals are used. You need this page and nothing else.

Learning objectives

You can:

  • match the formula, the structural formula, the 2D view and the 3D view of the same species;
  • read a wedge-and-dash drawing;
  • name the shape at an atom and give the size of each marked bond angle.

Key ideas

The 2D view is the displayed formula. It shows every atom and every bond, drawn flat on the page, so the angles on the page are not the real angles. The 3D view shows the real shape. In the 3D view a plain line lies in the page, a solid wedge comes towards you, and a dashed wedge goes away from you.

An ion has square brackets and an overall charge in the corner. A small + or − beside an atom is the charge on that atom. A letter such as a on a 3D view marks an angle you must work out. A flat species, such as carbon dioxide or sulfur trioxide, looks the same in both views because every atom lies in the plane of the page.

Underlying ideas: definitions to learn

formula
The number of atoms of each element in one molecule or ion.
structural formula
The atoms written in the order they are joined, with groups kept together.
displayed formula
A flat 2D drawing of every atom and every bond. The angles on the page are not the real angles.
3D view
A drawing that uses plain lines, solid wedges and dashed wedges to show the real shape.
wedge
A solid wedge is a bond coming towards you. A dashed wedge is a bond going away from you. A plain line lies in the page.
ion
An atom or group of atoms with an overall charge, shown in square brackets with the charge in the corner.
bond angle
The angle between two bonds that meet at the same atom.
shape
The 3D arrangement of the atoms joined to a central atom.
tetrahedral
Four atoms joined to the centre, pointing to the corners of a tetrahedron. Bond angle 109.5 degrees.
trigonal planar
Three atoms joined to the centre, all in one flat plane. Bond angle 120 degrees.
trigonal pyramidal
Three atoms joined to the centre, forming a low pyramid. Bond angle 107 degrees.
bent
Two atoms joined to the centre at an angle of about 104.5 degrees.
linear
Atoms in a straight line. Bond angle 180 degrees.
