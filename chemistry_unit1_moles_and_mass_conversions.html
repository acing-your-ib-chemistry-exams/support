Glossary

Relative atomic mass (Ar)
The mass of one atom of an element, compared with 1/12th the mass of a carbon-12 atom. Read straight off the periodic table.
Molar mass (Mr, molecular mass)
The mass of one mole of a substance, measured in grams per mole (g/mol), found by adding together the relative atomic masses of all the atoms in its formula.
Mole
The unit chemists use for an amount of substance — one mole of anything always contains the same, very large, fixed number of particles (atoms, molecules or formula units).
Bracket multiplier
A subscript written after a closing bracket, which multiplies every atom inside that bracket, for example the 2 in Ca(NO₃)₂.
Criss-cross method
A way of writing a balanced ionic formula: the size of the charge on the cation becomes the subscript on the anion, and the size of the charge on the anion becomes the subscript on the cation, then the ratio is simplified if possible.

C. The mole–mass relationship new idea

A moleThe unit chemists use for an amount of substance — one mole of anything always contains the same, very large, fixed number of particles. is just an amount — like a "dozen" is always 12 of something, a mole is always the same enormous number of particles. What links moles and mass is the molar mass (Mr) you've just been practising.

mass (g)
moles (mol)
Mr (g/mol)

Cover the one you want to find. Mass on top of moles and Mr means: mass = moles × Mr, or moles = mass ÷ Mr.

Worked example — moles to mass How many grams are in 2 mol of CaCl₂ (Mr = 111)?
  1. mass = moles × Mr = 2 × 111 = 222
Mass = 222 g.
Worked example — mass to moles How many moles are in 22.2 g of CaCl₂ (Mr = 111)?
  1. moles = mass ÷ Mr = 22.2 ÷ 111 = 0.2
Moles = 0.2 mol.

Bonus round stretch

A harder look at the same idea: counting atoms within a formula, and a two-step problem linking two different compounds.

29. Calcium carbonate, CaCO₃, contains 3 oxygen atoms in every formula unit. How many moles of oxygen atoms are there in 2 mol of CaCO₃?

HintMultiply the moles of CaCO₃ by the number of oxygens in each formula unit.
Model answer (advanced)2 mol × 3 = 6 mol of oxygen atoms — the same subscript-multiplying logic from your molar mass calculations, just counting moles of atoms instead of grams.

30. What mass of magnesium chloride, MgCl₂ (Mr = 95), contains the same number of moles as 4 g of sodium hydroxide, NaOH (Mr = 40)?

HintTwo steps: first find the moles of NaOH, then use that number of moles to find the MgCl₂ mass.
Model answer (advanced)4 ÷ 40 = 0.1 mol of NaOH; 0.1 × 95 = 9.5 g of MgCl₂ — the same number of moles, but a very different mass, since the two compounds have different molar masses.

31. A sneak peek at where this leads next: MgCO₃ + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂. The "2" in front of HCl means every 1 mol of MgCO₃ needs 2 mol of HCl to react completely. If you have 0.2 mol of MgCO₃, how many moles of HCl do you need?

HintDouble the moles of MgCO₃.
Model answer (advanced)0.2 × 2 = 0.4 mol — using ratios straight from a balanced equation like this is exactly what full stoichiometry calculations build on, once you've mastered moles and mass.
