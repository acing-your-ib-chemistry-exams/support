Part B

B1The slow reaction makes I2; the fast reaction removes it. The delay with no colour shows iodine is removed as fast as it is made; the sudden change shows the fast reaction has stopped.
B2–B3The thiosulfate converts I2 back to I−, so no free I2 reaches the starch until the thiosulfate is exhausted. Afterwards the free iodine appears at once and turns the starch blue-black, so the change is sudden.
B4H2O2(aq) + 2S2O32−(aq) + 2H+(aq) → S4O62−(aq) + 2H2O(l)
B5Iodide: used in the slow reaction, regenerated in the fast one, so it behaves as a catalyst until the thiosulfate runs out.
B62 mol.

Part F

F140 s. Twice the thiosulfate means twice the iodine to be made; the rate is unchanged because thiosulfate is not in the slow step. Time = amountrate doubles.
F2About 20 s, unchanged. Halving A halves [I−], so the rate halves, but it also halves the thiosulfate to be used up, so the time cancels out. This is a useful discussion point: diluting A cannot show an iodide effect. To investigate iodide, change only the KI in A.
F3Any two with reasoning, for example: (i) lower acidity in diluted B (a buffer estimate, not measured, puts the pH nearer 4.4 at 2 cm³ of B than 3.7 at 10 cm³) is systematic, and any [H+]-dependent term would make the low-concentration points deviate; (ii) weaker peroxide shifts every concentration, a systematic error in the x-axis and in k; (iii) temperature drift gives scatter or a trend with run order; (iv) the eye detects blue-black slightly after the end point, adding a roughly constant delay, which matters most for the shortest times.

Notes on the specimen data

The times in Part E are not measurements. They are calculated from rate = k[H2O2][I−] with k = 7.0 × 10−3 dm³ mol−1 s−1, chosen so that the standard mixture gives 20 s as in the RSC demonstration, and rounded to 1 s. Real class data will scatter about the line, and may curve slightly because diluting B also changes the acidity (F3). Please substitute measured times when you have them.
