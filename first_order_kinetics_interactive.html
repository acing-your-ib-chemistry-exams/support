First-order reactions, the Maxwell–Boltzmann curve and rate

Brilliant blue dye and bleach (sodium chlorate(I)), followed by colorimetry. Hover over, or tap, a dotted term for its meaning; every term is in the glossary at the end.

Learning objectives and key ideas

  • Recognise first-order behaviour from a decay curve and from a constant half-life.
  • Link the half-life to the rate constant, k, and use the exponential decay equation.
  • Explain with the Maxwell–Boltzmann curve how temperature and a catalyst change the rate.

Explorer 2: temperature, concentration and a catalyst

Left: the Maxwell–Boltzmann curve (energy of the molecules). Right: the dye absorbance that results. The grey dashed curves are the starting conditions (25 °C, bleach 0.010 mol dm⁻³, no catalyst, half-life 150 s). Ea starts at 50 kJ mol⁻¹, a typical value, so the graph has a break: the curve to scale on the left, the tail magnified on the right. Press Pin to keep the current curve as a new comparison. This model uses the exact fraction of molecules at or above the activation energy; collisions also become slightly more frequent when hotter, which it ignores.

Left of the break the curve is drawn to scale. After the break the vertical scale is magnified, because the real tail beyond a typical activation energy is a tiny fraction of the molecules. The shaded area is the fraction of all molecules with energy at or above Ea: the molecules able to react.

Underlying ideas: definitions to learn

termdefinition
absorbancehow much light a coloured solution absorbs; proportional to the concentration of the coloured substance.
first-order reactiona reaction whose rate is proportional to the concentration of one reactant; the half-life is constant.
exponential decaya quantity that falls by the same fraction in each equal interval of time.
half-life, t½the time for the concentration of a reactant to fall to half its value.
rate constant, kthe constant in rate = k × concentration; for first order it has units s⁻¹ and equals 0.693 over the half-life.
pseudo-first-orderbehaving as first order because the other reactant is in such large excess that its concentration stays almost constant.
activation energy, Eathe minimum energy colliding particles need to react.
Maxwell–Boltzmann distributionthe spread of energies of molecules at one temperature; the area is the total number of molecules.
catalystspeeds up a reaction by giving a route with lower activation energy; not used up.
