FIS · Science Department · draft scheme of work

Grade 9 Chemistry — week by week

One semester, 19 weeks, meeting Monday, Wednesday and Friday — 57 sessions. Practical work is the spine rather than the illustration: most weeks carry at least one session at the bench, and the explanation is built afterwards from what the class actually measured.

Unit 1 · weeks 1–4

Foundations: formulae, measurement and the periodic table

Week1

Monday

Course start — safety induction, bench routines, apparatus.

Burning magnesium ribbon as a first exercise in recording what you see, not what you expect.

WednesdayPractical

Preparing magnesium carbonate.

Making a precipitate. Observation discipline; every change written at the bench, not afterwards.

FridayPractical

Purifying magnesium carbonate.

Filtration, washing, drying to constant mass. Yield recorded, losses accounted for.

Week2

Monday

Elements, compounds and mixtures — names and symbols.

Vocabulary page opened and added to from today onwards.

Wednesday

Ionic charges → formulae.

Common ions sheet. Drilled until it is automatic, because everything downstream assumes it.

Friday

Word equations → symbol equations → balancing.

Conservation of mass argued from the Week 1 magnesium result.

Week3

Monday

The mole: mass, Mr, and counting particles.

Why chemists count in moles rather than grams.

WednesdayPractical

Reacting masses: mass change on heating.

Full data set collected, repeated where time allows.

Friday

Moles from your own data; percentage yield.

Moles, equations and formulae booklet started.

Week4

Monday

The periodic table as a prediction tool.

Groups 1, 7 and 0. Open University video and notes.

WednesdayPractical

Electrolysis — observations at each electrode.

Then aluminium extraction (RSC film) as the industrial case. Park the question of what is moving; it returns in Week 18.

FridayAssessment

Checkpoint: formulae, moles and analysis.

Marked and reviewed in the same lesson.

Unit 2 · weeks 5–8

Thermochemistry and ΔH

NGSS HS-PS1-4 — develop a model of energy release and absorption

Week5

MondayPractical

Exothermic and endothermic — a circus of reactions.

Thermometer in hand, six benches, no explanations offered yet.

WednesdayPractical

Temperature change of neutralisation and of dissolving.

The sign convention for ΔH established from the class's own data rather than announced.

Friday

Energy level diagrams drawn from your results.

Diagram must match the data you personally collected.

Week6

Monday

Calorimetry: q = mcΔT.

Where the assumptions sit, and which of them you can test.

WednesdayPractical

Enthalpy of combustion of an alcohol.

Spirit burner, full data set, masses before and after.

Friday

Calculating ΔH, then comparing with the data book.

Accounting for the gap: heat lost to the room, incomplete combustion, draughts.

Week7

Monday

Bonds broken, bonds made — building the model.

Molecular-level representation of where the energy goes. Modelling task.

Wednesday

Bond enthalpies: calculating ΔH from bond energies.

And why the values are averages rather than measurements of your reaction.

Friday

Modelled, calculated, measured — three ΔH values for one reaction.

Which do you trust, and what would change your mind?

Week8

Monday

Unit review; self-assessment sheet.

Error log started — you will keep it all semester.

WednesdayAssessment

Checkpoint: calculations and thermochemistry.

Friday

Checkpoint review; combustion write-up submitted.

Feedback read and acted on before it is filed.

Unit 3 · weeks 9–12

Rates of reaction (kinetics)

NGSS HS-PS1-5 — explain the effect of temperature and concentration on rate

Week9

Monday

What makes a reaction fast?

Collision theory built up from what you saw in Unit 2, not handed over ready-made.

WednesdayPractical

Marble chips and acid: surface area.

Gas collection or mass loss — you choose the method and justify it.

Friday

Rate from gradients; graphing in Logger Pro.

Tangents, units, and what a curve flattening actually means.

Week10

Monday

Activation energy; the energy profile revisited.

The same diagram as Unit 2, now with a hill in it.

WednesdayPractical

Sodium thiosulfate and acid: concentration.

The disappearing cross. One variable changed, everything else held.

Friday

Analysing the concentration data; rate ∝ 1/t.

Explanation written as evidence → reasoning → claim.

Week11

Monday

Temperature, and the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution.

PhET gas properties: watch the tail of the curve, not the average.

WednesdayPractical

Thiosulfate again — this time varying temperature.

Same method, one variable moved. Comparison is the point.

Friday

Explaining the temperature effect properly.

“Particles move faster” is not an explanation. The curve is.

Week12

Monday

Catalysts: what they change, and what they leave alone.

A distinction that matters again in Week 15.

WednesdayPractical

Catalysed decomposition of hydrogen peroxide.

Rate measured, catalyst recovered and weighed.

FridayAssessment

Checkpoint: kinetics.

Checklist 6.1.7 and Ask Wilko available beforehand.

Unit 4 · weeks 13–16

Reversible reactions and equilibrium

NGSS HS-PS1-6 — refine a chemical system to increase product at equilibrium

Week13

MondayPractical

Reversible reactions — hydrated and anhydrous copper sulfate.

Observed in both directions, in one lesson.

WednesdayPractical

Closed systems and open systems.

Why the lid changes the chemistry.

Friday

Dynamic equilibrium: still reacting, no longer changing.

Our equilibrium model simulation used to make the idea concrete.

Week14

Monday

Kc — writing the expression, reading the number.

What a large Kc tells you and what it does not.

Wednesday

Calculations with Kc; the reaction quotient.

Predicting which way a system will move before it moves.

Friday

Practice and feedback.

Common errors surfaced deliberately and corrected in class.

Week15

Monday

Le Chatelier's principle: concentration and pressure.

Stated carefully, including what it does not predict.

WednesdayPractical

Shifting an equilibrium you can watch.

Predict on paper first, then test. Cobalt chloride or chromate–dichromate.

Friday

Temperature and equilibrium.

The link straight back to the sign of ΔH from Unit 2.

Week16

Monday

Industrial equilibria: the Haber process.

RSC ammonia film. Yield, rate and cost pulling in three directions.

Wednesday

Haber simulation — refining the conditions.

Specify one change, justify it, record the effect. Worksheet completed.

FridayAssessment

Checkpoint: equilibrium.

Practice Checkpoint released the week before.

Unit 5 · weeks 17–19

Reduction and oxidation

Week17

Monday

Oxidation and reduction — from oxygen to electrons.

The definition widens twice in one lesson.

WednesdayPractical

Displacement reactions.

A reactivity order built from your own observations, then compared with the published one.

Friday

Oxidation numbers: the rules.

Interactive activity; drilled to fluency.

Week18

Monday

Half-equations, and combining them into a whole.

Electrons balanced as carefully as atoms.

Wednesday

Oxidising and reducing agents.

Re-reading the semester's earlier reactions with new eyes.

Friday

Electrochemistry: cells that use a reaction, cells that force one.

The aluminium cell from Week 4 finally explained.

Week19

Monday

Synoptic review.

One industrial process, explained using energetics, kinetics, equilibrium and redox together.

WednesdayAssessment

End-of-course paper.

Friday

Paper review; Grade 10 course choice.

Medicinal Chemistry or Industrial Chemistry — questions answered.

Draft for discussion — sequence and pacing are a proposal, not a fixed plan. Five Checkpoints, one per unit, plus a synoptic paper in Week 19.
