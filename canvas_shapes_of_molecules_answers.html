Part C. A flat picture, or the real shape?

A displayed formula is a map of which atoms are joined to which. It is drawn flat on paper, so it cannot show the real shape. Methane, CH4, shows the difference.

In the three-dimensional drawing, three kinds of line show depth:

LineWhat it shows
plain linea bond in the plane of the page
solid wedgea bond coming towards you
dashed wedgea bond going away from you

C1. In the flat displayed formula of methane, the H–C–H angles look like 90°. In the real molecule they are 109.5°.

C2. Why is a displayed formula still worth drawing, even though it does not show the shape?

It shows exactly which atoms are joined and whether each bond is single, double or triple. The shape is predicted from that information.

Part D. Predict the shape and the bond angle

Electron groups around a central atom push each other as far apart as they can. A group is a bond (single, double or triple, each counts as one group) or a lone pair. Count the groups, then use the chart. A two-atom molecule such as HCl has one bond only, so it has no bond angle.

Underlying ideas: definitions to learn

Covalent bond
A shared pair of electrons between two atoms.
Molecular formula
The number of atoms of each element in one molecule.
Structural formula
The atoms written in a line in the order they are joined, with groups kept together.
Displayed formula
A flat drawing of every atom and every bond in a molecule.
Lone pair
A pair of outer electrons on an atom that is not used in bonding.
Group
One bond (single, double or triple) or one lone pair around an atom.
Bond angle
The angle between two bonds that meet at the same atom.
Shape
The three-dimensional arrangement of atoms in a molecule, set by groups pushing apart as far as possible.

Scale up: a molecule with three centres

Ethanol has nine atoms and three atoms that each have more than one bond: two carbon atoms and one oxygen atom. Each has its own shape and bond angle.

The central atoms of methane, ammonia and water have 0, 1 and 2 lone pairs, and their bond angles are 109.5°, 107° and 104.5°. Write one sentence that links the number of lone pairs to the size of the angle.

Lone pairs take up more room around the atom than bonding pairs, so each lone pair squeezes the bonds closer together and the bond angle gets smaller.

Notes for the teacher

  • Measured values differ a little from the model: H–C–H in methanal is about 116°, in ethanol the C–O–H angle is close to 108°. The values on the chart are the simple-model values and are the ones to accept.
  • Part A: structural formulae of the form CH3CH3, CH3OH, CH2=CH2, HC≡CH, CH3CH2Cl and O=C=O are all acceptable; molecular formulae follow the order C, H, then other elements.
  • Part D, hydrogen cyanide: the central atom is carbon, joined to H and to N, so two groups and a linear shape. The triple bond is one group.
