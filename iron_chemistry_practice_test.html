Part A  Iron chemistry

Learning objectives

In Part A you show that you can:

  1. write the formulae of iron(II) and iron(III) compounds by balancing the charges on their ions;
  2. recognise the colours of the precipitates iron(II) hydroxide, iron(II) carbonate and iron(III) hydroxide;
  3. complete a full equation, with state symbols, for a [[precipitation reaction]];
  4. identify the [[spectator ion|spectator ions]] and write the [[ionic equation]];
  5. explain why green iron(II) hydroxide turns orange-brown in air, and use precipitates to tell iron(II) ions from iron(III) ions;
  6. calculate the mass of a precipitate from the volume and concentration of a solution;
  7. explain the [[displacement]] of copper by an iron nail in copper(II) sulfate solution, and write its ionic equation.

Facts you need
  • All sodium compounds are soluble: they stay dissolved, (aq).
  • Iron(II) and iron(III) sulfates, chlorides and nitrates are soluble: (aq).
  • Iron hydroxides and iron(II) carbonate are insoluble: they are the precipitates, (s).

Cheat sheet: an ionic equation in four small steps
  1. Write the full equation with state symbols.
  2. Split every (aq) ionic compound into its ions. Keep the (s) precipitate in one piece.
  3. Cross out the ions that are identical on both sides: these are the spectator ions.
  4. Write what is left. Check that the atoms balance and that the total charge is the same on both sides.

Worked example with different substances:

Mg2+(aq) + 2OH−(aq) → Mg(OH)2(s)
