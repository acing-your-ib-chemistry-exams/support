Cheat sheet: formulae of ionic compounds
  1. Find each ion in the ion table and note its charge.
  2. Make the total positive charge equal the total negative charge. Use the fewest ions you can.
  3. Write the metal first. Write the number of each ion as a small number after its symbol. Write nothing for 1.
  4. If you need two or more of a group of atoms such as OH, NO3, SO4, CO3 or PO4, put the group in brackets before the number: Ca(OH)2.
  5. A Roman numeral in a name, as in iron(III), tells you which charge the metal ion has.
  6. Do not write the charges in the formula.

Example: aluminium sulfate. Al3+ and SO42−. 2 × 3+ = 6+ and 3 × 2− = 6−, so 2 Al and 3 SO4: Al2(SO4)3.

Section B: the molecular world

Molecular compounds are made of non-metals only. Their atoms are joined by bonds into small molecules. You can build them with the modelling kits, work out their formulae and draw them. Use the rules below.

Cheat sheet: the bonding rules
AtomCHClNO
Bonds41132
  • These rules hold most of the time. Use them to work out how many atoms join together, and to draw the molecule.
  • In a drawing, each line is one bond. Two lines are a double bond and three lines are a triple bond. A double bond uses 2 of each atom’s bonds.
  • Every atom must have its full number of bonds, and all the atoms must be joined into one molecule.
  • Prefixes in names: mono = 1, di = 2, tri = 3, tetra = 4. For example, carbon tetrachloride has 4 chlorine atoms.

The numbers on your drawing are the angles between the bonds at each atom. Aim for the angles in the last column for a flat drawing. Use the Real angle column in the 3D questions. Learn the names of the shapes in the second column. Angles within about 10° of them are accepted.

Why do the last two rows differ from the real angles? A flat page cannot show a pyramid or a four-bond shape in three dimensions, so we spread the bonds evenly round the atom instead.

Keep the atoms apart. If a bond passes through another atom, it turns red.

The shape names the arrangement of the atoms round one atom. A small atom in the sketch is joined to the grey centre atom. The solid wedge comes towards you and the dashed line goes away from you.

Nitrogen in NH3 and oxygen in H2O have electrons that are not used in bonds. They push the bonds closer together, so the angles are a little smaller than 109.5°.

A molecule with more than one centre atom has a shape round each one. Ask about one atom at a time.

B6 Molar mass

Cheat sheet: molar mass
  1. Count the atoms of each element in the formula. No number means 1.
  2. Multiply each count by the Ar of that element.
  3. Add the masses. The unit of molar mass is g mol−1.

Example: CH4: 1 × 12 + 4 × 1 = 16 g mol−1.

Underlying ideas: definitions to learn

Bond
A link between two atoms. In a drawing it is a line. A double bond is two lines and a triple bond is three lines.
Formula
The symbols of the elements in a compound, with small numbers to show how many atoms (or ions) of each there are.
Ion
An atom or group of atoms with an electric charge. Metals form positive ions. Many non-metals and groups such as OH and SO4 form negative ions.
Ionic compound
A compound made of positive and negative ions, usually a metal joined to a non-metal. The total positive charge equals the total negative charge.
Molar mass, M
The mass of one mole of a substance, in g mol−1. Add up number of atoms × Ar for every element in the formula. It was once called the molecular mass.
Molecular compound
A compound made of molecules, which are small groups of atoms joined by bonds. Molecular compounds are made only of non-metals.
Relative atomic mass, Ar
The mass of an atom of an element compared with other atoms. We use whole numbers, except chlorine (35.5).
