Adventures with a CFSTR

Valves, a steam jacket, and a tank you can fill, drain and heat

Learning objectives

  • Explain how the opening of each valve sets a flow rate, and how a difference between the flow in and the flow out makes the liquid volume rise or fall.
  • Describe steady state and explain why the dye concentration in the tank settles at a constant value.
  • Predict how tank volume, flow rates and temperature change the average residence time and the dye concentration in the output stream.
  • Use the first-order CFSTR relationship to calculate the output concentration.

The Exploration

One stream is bleach. The other is water carrying BBD. Each of the two inlets and the output has a valve, so you decide the flow rate in every pipe. Steam flowing through the steam jacket warms the tank, and a fourth valve controls the steam. The tank starts at steady state, with a flow in equal to the flow out. Change a valve and watch the tank respond.

Writing a good scientific answer

  • Answer the question in the first sentence, then give the reason.
  • Use the terms of the topic: steady state, residence time, rate constant, flow rate in, flow rate out.
  • Support a claim with a number and its unit, taken from the simulation or from a calculation.
  • Link ideas with because, so and therefore, and keep to one idea in each sentence.
  • Say what increases or decreases, and say what stays the same.
WEAK It gets too hot, so it stops.
BETTER The temperature levels off because the heat arriving from the steam equals the heat leaving with the output stream and through the tank wall. Nothing in the tank then changes with time, which is steady state.

Underlying ideas: definitions to learn

CFSTR
A continuous flow stirred tank reactor. Liquid flows in and out all the time, and a stirrer keeps the contents the same everywhere.
Valve opening
How far a valve is open, in percent. In this model every 1 % of opening passes 2 cm³ per minute, so a fully open valve passes 200 cm³ per minute.
Steady state
Nothing in the tank changes with time. The flow in equals the flow out, so the volume is constant, and the concentration and temperature are constant too.
Perfectly mixed
The concentration and temperature are identical at every point in the tank, so the output stream has the same concentration and temperature as the tank.
Average residence time
The average time a portion of liquid spends in the tank. In one residence time, a volume of liquid equal to the liquid in the tank has flowed out.
Steam jacket
A hollow wall around the tank. Steam at 100 °C condenses on the inner wall, and the heat released warms the liquid. The condensate leaves at the bottom.
First-order reaction
A reaction whose rate is proportional to the concentration of one substance. Here the bleach is present in such large excess that its concentration stays constant, so the dye disappears at a rate proportional to the dye concentration alone.
Rate constant for dye removal
The number that tells you how fast the dye disappears. A larger value means faster removal. It grows with the hypochlorite concentration and with temperature.

Change in liquid volume (positive means the tank is filling):

change in liquid volume per minute = total flow rate in − output flow rate

BBD concentration in the output stream = BBD concentration from mixing alone1 + rate constant for dye removal × average residence time

Values built into the model

  • Both feeds arrive at 20 °C, and the surroundings are at 20 °C.
  • Steam is at 100 °C. The fully open steam valve would supply 10 g of steam per minute. Each gram of steam condensing releases 2257 J, and the jacket can pass only as much heat as the temperature difference allows, so the steam actually condensing is less than the supply when the tank is hot.
  • The rate constant for the reaction of BBD with hypochlorite is 0.8 dm³ mol⁻¹ min⁻¹ at 20 °C, with an activation energy of 50 kJ mol⁻¹, so the rate roughly doubles for every 10 °C rise.
  • The liquid is treated as water: density 1 g cm⁻³ and specific heat capacity 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹.
