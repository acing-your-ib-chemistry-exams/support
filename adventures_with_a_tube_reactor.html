Adventures with a tube reactor

Plug flow, valves, and a steam jacket along the tube

Learning objectives

  • Describe a plug flow reactor and the assumption that every portion of liquid spends exactly the same time in the tube.
  • Calculate the volume of a tube, the speed of the liquid and the average residence time.
  • Use the first-order plug flow relationship to predict the BBD concentration in the output stream.
  • Compare a tube with a CFSTR of the same volume.

The Exploration

The same two streams as before meet in a mixer: bleach, and water carrying BBD. The mixture then flows through a long, narrow tube. We assume plug flow: the liquid moves along as plugs that do not mix with each other. Each plug acts like a small batch of liquid that reacts for exactly as long as it takes to travel the tube. A valve controls each inlet, a valve controls the output, and a steam jacket with its own valve warms the whole tube. The tube is always full, so the flow in always equals the flow out. If the output valve passes less than the two inlets would supply, both inlet flows are reduced in the same proportion. The tube starts at steady state.

Writing a good scientific answer

  • Answer the question in the first sentence, then give the reason.
  • Use the terms of the topic: steady state, residence time, rate constant, flow rate in, flow rate out.
  • Support a claim with a number and its unit, taken from the simulation or from a calculation.
  • Link ideas with because, so and therefore, and keep to one idea in each sentence.
  • Say what increases or decreases, and say what stays the same.
WEAK It gets too hot, so it stops.
BETTER The temperature levels off because the heat arriving from the steam equals the heat leaving with the output stream and through the tank wall. Nothing in the tank then changes with time, which is steady state.

Underlying ideas: definitions to learn

Plug flow reactor
A tube in which the liquid moves as plugs. There is no mixing along the tube, so each plug spends exactly the same time in it, and there is perfect mixing across the tube, so each plug has one concentration and one temperature.
Average residence time
The time a plug spends in the tube. In a plug flow reactor every plug spends exactly this time. A plug is a tiny batch reactor that travels along the tube.
Steady state
Nothing at any point along the tube changes with time. The concentration and temperature differ from place to place along the tube, but each place keeps the same values.
First-order reaction
A reaction whose rate is proportional to the concentration of one substance. Here the bleach is in such large excess that its concentration stays constant, so the dye disappears at a rate proportional to the dye concentration alone.
Steam jacket
A hollow wall around the tube. Steam condenses on the inner tube and the heat released warms the liquid. The condensate leaves at the bottom.

Values built into the model

  • Each 1 % of valve opening passes 2 cm³ per minute, so a fully open valve passes 200 cm³ per minute.
  • Both feeds arrive at 20 °C. The mixer mixes the two streams completely before the liquid enters the tube.
  • Steam is at 100 °C. The fully open steam valve would supply 10 g of steam per minute, and each gram of steam condensing releases 2257 J. If the supply is smaller than the tube could take, the jacket temperature falls below 100 °C. The steam-to-liquid heat transfer is 300 W for each square metre of tube wall for each kelvin of temperature difference, and heat losses to the surroundings are ignored.
  • The rate constant for the reaction of BBD with hypochlorite is 0.8 dm³ mol⁻¹ min⁻¹ at 20 °C, with an activation energy of 50 kJ mol⁻¹, so the rate roughly doubles for every 10 °C rise.
  • The liquid is treated as water: density 1 g cm⁻³ and specific heat capacity 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹.
