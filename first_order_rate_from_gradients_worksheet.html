First-order reactions: rate from gradients, and rate against concentration

Brilliant blue dye and bleach (sodium chlorate(I)). You will use the Graphical Analysis Toolkit to turn a concentration–time curve into a graph that shows what first order means.

Learning objectives and key ideas

By the end of this worksheet you can:

  • plot concentration against time with the toolkit and place tangents at five points;
  • use the gradient of each tangent as the rate of reaction at that moment;
  • plot rate (vertical axis) against concentration (horizontal axis), use a linear fit, and say what the straight line through the origin proves;
  • find the rate constant, k, from the gradient of that line.

Key ideas

gradient = change in concentrationchange in time

The concentration of dye falls, so every gradient of the concentration–time graph is negative. The rate of reaction is the size of the gradient: write it as a positive number.

rate of reaction = rate constant, k × concentration of dye, c

A reaction with this rate equation is first order in the dye. A graph of rate against concentration is then a straight line through the origin, with gradient k.

The curve falls steeply at the start (high concentration) and becomes less and less steep, approaching zero but never reaching it: an exponential decay. The steepness (the rate) is greater when there is more dye.

Part D: what the graph shows

Underlying ideas: definitions to learn

termdefinition
concentration, cthe amount of a substance in a given volume of solution; here in µmol dm⁻³.
gradienthow steep a line is: the change in the vertical value over the change in the horizontal value.
tangenta straight line that just touches a curve at one point, with the same steepness as the curve there.
rate of reactionhow fast the concentration of a reactant falls: the size of the gradient of its concentration–time graph.
proportionalwhen one quantity doubles, the other doubles; a graph of one against the other is a straight line through the origin.
first-order reactiona reaction whose rate is proportional to the concentration of one reactant: rate of reaction = rate constant, k × concentration.
rate constant, kthe constant in the rate equation; for first order it is the gradient of a graph of rate against concentration, with unit s⁻¹, and equals 0.693 over the half-life.
half-lifethe time for the concentration of a reactant to fall to half its value.
